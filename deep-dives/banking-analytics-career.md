# Breaking Into Banking Analytics: The Honest Career Guide

*From MBA to model governance — what actually matters, what doesn't, and what nobody tells you*

**Published:** March 2026 | **Reading time:** 9 min | **Topic:** Career in Banking Analytics

---

## Who This Is For

You're finishing an MBA in Analytics, or a B.Tech with data science skills, and you're wondering: should I go into banking analytics? What do these roles actually look like? What skills do I actually need?

I went from NIT Durgapur (B.Tech, Electrical Engineering) → Alstom (Project Management) → IIM Kashipur (MBA Analytics) → Bank of America (Assistant Manager, Model Governance). Here's what I've learned about the banking analytics career path — with zero sugar-coating.

## The Banking Analytics Landscape

Not all "analytics" roles in banks are the same. Here's the actual hierarchy:

```
Banking Analytics Roles (by complexity):

Tier 1: Reporting & BI
├── MIS Analyst, BI Developer, Reporting Analyst
├── Tools: SQL, Excel, Tableau, Power BI
├── Work: Building dashboards, scheduled reports, data pulls
└── Entry-level, high volume hiring

Tier 2: Business Analytics
├── Business Analyst, Product Analyst, Strategy Analyst
├── Tools: SQL, Python/R, Tableau, Excel
├── Work: Customer segmentation, campaign analysis, A/B tests
└── Requires business acumen + technical skills

Tier 3: Risk & Fraud Analytics
├── Risk Analyst, Fraud Analyst, Credit Risk Modeler
├── Tools: SAS, Python, SQL, Hive, statistical modeling
├── Work: Building/validating scorecards, PD/LGD models, fraud detection
└── Highest regulatory scrutiny, most specialized

Tier 4: Model Risk Management / Governance
├── Model Validation Analyst, Model Governance Analyst
├── Tools: SAS, Python, SQL + deep regulatory knowledge
├── Work: Independent validation, SR 11-7 compliance, model monitoring
└── Hardest to break into, highest strategic impact
```

**The higher the tier, the harder to automate — and the better the career trajectory.**

## What Banks Actually Look For

### The Skills That Get You Hired

| Skill | Importance | Why |
|-------|-----------|-----|
| **SQL** | Critical | Every banking analytics role runs on SQL. Not optional. |
| **SAS** | High (for now) | Legacy systems everywhere. Banks are migrating to Python but SAS is still dominant. |
| **Python** | High (and rising) | ML models, automation, data pipelines. The future of banking analytics. |
| **Tableau/Power BI** | Medium-High | Every team needs dashboards. Quick way to demonstrate value. |
| **Statistics** | Critical | Hypothesis testing, regression, distributions. You need to think statistically. |
| **Domain knowledge** | Differentiator | Understanding SR 11-7, BSA/AML, CCAR/DFAST, fraud typologies separates analytics people from data people. |

### The Skills They List But Don't Actually Test

- Machine learning (they say they want it, but 80% of bank models are logistic regression)
- Deep learning / Neural networks (almost never used in regulated banking models — too hard to explain)
- Hadoop / Spark (infrastructure teams handle this; you just query Hive)

### The Skills They Don't List But Desperately Need

- **Communication**: Can you explain model results to a non-technical business leader?
- **Documentation**: Can you write a Model Development Document that passes regulatory review?
- **Regulatory awareness**: Do you understand why banks can't just deploy any model they want?
- **Stakeholder management**: Can you work with model developers, validators, and business owners simultaneously?

## The Honest Career Timeline

### Year 0-1: Survive and Learn
- You will feel overwhelmed by acronyms: MRM, SR 11-7, OCC, FDIC, PSI, CSI, KS, Gini, CCAR, DFAST
- Your SAS/SQL skills matter more than your Python skills initially
- Build one thing that saves your team time (I automated model performance reporting with SAS + Tableau)
- Say yes to every project that touches model validation or regulatory work

### Year 1-3: Specialize
- Pick a lane: fraud, credit risk, operational risk, or AML
- Get deep in one regulatory framework (SR 11-7 for model risk, BSA for AML)
- Start owning model monitoring for specific model portfolios
- Build relationships with model validators — they see across the entire model inventory

### Year 3-5: Own a Domain
- You should be the go-to person for a specific model family or risk domain
- Lead model revalidation exercises
- Present findings to model risk committees
- Mentor junior analysts
- Start thinking about VP-level responsibilities: model inventory strategy, technology roadmap, regulatory response

### Year 5+: Strategic Leadership
- Model risk strategy for the business unit
- Regulatory examination management
- Technology decisions (SAS to Python migration, cloud adoption)
- Building and leading teams

## The Compensation Reality

Transparent numbers for US banking analytics (2025-2026, major banks):

```
Analyst / Associate:          $75K - $100K + 10-15% bonus
Senior Analyst / AVP:         $100K - $140K + 15-20% bonus
VP (Model Risk / Fraud):      $140K - $190K + 20-30% bonus
Director / SVP:               $190K - $260K + 30-50% bonus
Managing Director:            $260K+ with significant variable comp
```

**Reality check:** Banking pays well but not tech-well. A senior ML engineer at a FAANG makes more than a VP in banking analytics. The tradeoff: banking has more stability, clearer career ladders, and interesting regulatory complexity that tech doesn't have.

## What I'd Do Differently

Looking back on my own path:

1. **Learn SAS earlier**: I underestimated how dominant SAS still is in banking. My Python skills were great for interviews, but day one on the job was SAS.

2. **Study regulations before starting**: Reading SR 11-7 and OCC 2011-12 before my first day would have saved weeks of confusion.

3. **Build a portfolio of banking-relevant projects**: Generic ML projects don't impress banking hiring managers. Build a credit scorecard, a fraud detection model, or a model monitoring dashboard.

4. **Network within the regulatory ecosystem**: The model risk management community is small. People at Fed, OCC, and FDIC conferences are the same people who validate your models.

5. **Write about what you're learning**: Publishing analysis on banking topics (which I now do) establishes credibility and creates inbound opportunities.

## The Entry Points

### If You're in an MBA Program
- Target rotational programs at large banks (they cycle you through different analytics teams)
- Case competitions with banking/fintech focus (Samsung EDGE, Avalon Consulting) build credibility
- Summer internships in risk/fraud analytics convert to full-time at high rates

### If You're a B.Tech with No MBA
- Start in Tier 1/2 roles (MIS, BI, Business Analytics) at banks or banking tech firms
- Get SAS certified (SAS Base + Advanced)
- Move laterally into risk/fraud teams after 1-2 years
- Some banks (like BofA, JPM) have dedicated tech-to-risk career paths

### If You're Switching from Another Industry
- Your domain expertise from previous industry + new analytics skills = differentiated profile
- My Alstom experience (project management, cost optimization, risk budgets) directly translated to banking governance
- Highlight transferable skills: stakeholder management, cross-functional leadership, data-driven decision-making

## The Bottom Line

Banking analytics is one of the most underrated career paths in tech-adjacent roles. The work is intellectually demanding (you're literally building systems to prevent billions in fraud), well-compensated, and has clear progression. The barrier to entry is domain knowledge — not coding skills.

If you can combine technical proficiency (SQL, SAS/Python, statistics) with regulatory understanding (SR 11-7, BSA/AML) and communication skills (presenting to model risk committees), you'll be in the top 10% of candidates.

The people who thrive in banking analytics aren't the best coders — they're the best translators between business, technology, and regulation.
